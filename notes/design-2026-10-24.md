# Ten Years: design direction, from 24 October 2026

Claude leads design and code; Codex takes art, sound and voice from the brief at the top of
`prompts/frames.md`. The human reviews at milestones. This file says where the game is going and why,
so that any pass can be checked against it.

## What the game is

One winter, counted a day at a time, in which a man learns to tell a good day from a bad one. The
arrival and the boots are the model for everything: a few authored choices whose consequences come back
in ordinary screens, for weeks. The winter should feel authored all the way to the ice, not only in
its first hour.

## Principles

1. **Consequences arrive in ordinary screens.** New content changes what the breakfast, the work, the
   gate, the supper and the night say and offer. A new event is only worth adding if some routine
   screen remembers it.
2. **The economy is pressure, not a clock.** A man should not die of one choice. Big costs are spread
   over days, and every day with a choice in it. Skill is rewarded through the small levers (the second
   bowl, the trade, the sick day, the floors), not through surviving the drain.
3. **The eye, the brigade and the body must predict.** A meter the player cannot act on is noise.
4. **Words, not numbers.** The account says what took and what gave; the record says what the record
   does not say.
5. **The dry reversal is rationed.** One per screen at most; none in the mercies. Plain description
   carries the rest.
6. **Nothing is added that the sound and the picture cannot follow.** Every new screen names its frame
   and, where someone speaks, its voice line, so Codex can draw and record it.

## Pass 2: the second half of the winter (this pass)

The first hour is dense and the last two months are thin. This pass gives March to May its own
arc, with the people the player already knows.

- **Kuzma's fifteen years end on 1 May** (`beat kuzma_release`, day 119, morning and night). His number
  is read at the count; he goes to the office; in the evening he is back on his bunk with a paper:
  "held until special order". That night the player can sit with him, leave him be, or give him his
  tobacco. For a week the stove hears no stories (lines). The record says it.
- **Senka names a date** (`event senka_date`, April, partner and not gone): in the mud by the lean-to he
  draws the river and a sun and holds up ten fingers: the tenth, before the ice goes bad. The wire
  screen says so every night until then. If the player has not gone by the ninth, Senka goes alone
  (`beat senka_goes`, day 114, morning): the count comes out wrong; sometimes he is brought back on the
  fourth day, sometimes he is not found. The record says it either way.
- **The bathhouse** (`event banya`, every three weeks or so): the one hot thing in the camp. Go first and
  get the hot water, let the old men go first, or sell your turn to the urki for tobacco. Five clean
  days afterwards: the lice are quiet at night (a mercy more often), and the brigade remembers who
  went first.
- **The thaw under the boards** (in `beat thaw`): meltwater under Alyoshka's boards; half the kept bread is
  grey paste by morning. The lining is dry. (A nudge toward eating or going.)
- **The balance:** the small levers come down a little so that a player who has understood the economy
  reaches the ice about five winters in six, and the passive one about one in two.
- **The prose:** the weakest third of the dry reversals in the existing lines rewritten plainly.
- **Voice and art for Codex:** in `prompts/frames.md`, "For Codex: what is needed now".

## Pass 3, done: Pasha and the brigade as a story

The bunk by the door below "watched" (the cold costs more, the ladle last, a gap in the ring at noon),
the board and the tape above "one of us" (the percentage is yours to write; the inspector has a tape),
Pasha's letter to Vologda and the answer read to him twice. The work and supper screens read the state.

## Pass 4, done: Lubimov as a man, the bank, the hut's own sound

Lubimov asks what you were; a bookkeeper is fetched to add the bread ledger. The bank before the first
night: near, or across the ice where the dogs lose the trail. The night's soundscape follows the hut
(quiet after Kuzma's paper, draughty by the door) and the bathhouse has its own.

## Pass 5, done: Lev's death as a day, Moscow's answer, the fisherman

Lev's hundred grams kept or put in the pot; his pencil kept or left; the night after him. Moscow examines
the complaint and finds the bread correct, read out at noon; Pasha's letter can mention the man who told
Moscow the truth. The fisherman has a brother who had ten years: the truth earns dry foot cloths and
Grigory's name at the settlement.

## Later passes, in order of value

1. **A listening pass.** Nothing new has been heard by a person: the bathhouse, the quiet hut, the
   door, and Codex's recordings when they land. One evening with headphones, and a list.
2. **Lev's death as a story.** It is a morning and a line. The brigade keeps his bread; his boots go to
   the nearest man; Pasha says nothing. Give the day after it a shape, and let the record say what you
   did with his hundred grams.
3. **The escape's second half, again.** The fisherman's hut is one roll. Let the fisherman be a person
   (his own fence, his own children) and the settlement's man remember what the fisherman said.
4. **The commission's consequences.** Speaking to the commission is a cells day and the eye. It should
   also be a month: Lubimov's attention, the brigade's respect, a line in Pasha's letter.
5. **The second winter.** Only if the first is finished: the spring ending already points at it.

## Not doing

- No more meters, no numbers in prose, no tutorial text beyond Kuzma's answers.
- No new characters until the existing six have states that ordinary screens read.
- No rewriting of the arrival: it is the standard the rest is measured against.
